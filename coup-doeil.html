Flash the reference

Hides the model after a moment, so you answer from memory. It also makes finger-measuring impossible. Divisions are unaffected.

Eye, then pencil

Two passes: answer by eye first (this is what's scored and what moves the level). Then measure however you like, adjust, and check again to see how much the pencil recovers.

How to look
Segments and Copy ratio
Don't estimate the whole ratio at once. Lay the short length off along the long one in your mind: once, twice, then judge the remainder as a fraction of the unit. Halves and quarters of the unit are easy to see; thirds less so.
Shapes
Read the ellipse or rectangle as a box. Find its midpoint, then compare the half-height to the half-width. For a rotated shape, judge along its own axes, not the page's.
Angles
Compare to the nearest vertical or horizontal, then ask whether the deviation is closer to 15°, 30° or 45°. Inside a tilted frame, ignore the frame: the page edge is the only true vertical.
Divisions
Halve first, always. Thirds: find the point that looks like one part against two. Fifths: halve, then take the point that splits the larger half a little short of its middle.
Transfer
Locate the dot by two coordinates: how far across, how far down, each as a fraction of the shape. Then check against a diagonal or a corner.
Plumb and level
This is the plumb line without the string. Run a straight vertical or horizontal in your mind from the dot and note what it crosses. Errors creep in when the line is oblique; check against a page edge.
